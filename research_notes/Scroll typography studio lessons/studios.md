## What rules do they share on pin length, one idea per viewport, scrub versus entrance, and not exploding every word?

### Takeaway
There is no shared studio manifesto that says “one idea per viewport” or “never explode every word.” Those rules are not in the sources. What is written: Locomotive tells you to refuse scroll hijacking when the job is conversion or accessibility; GSAP treats a scrubbed timeline as ratios of one scroll distance, so a long multi-word assembly stays illegible until the last fraction of that distance. The strong pattern that matches both is a short, already-readable lockup, then at most one change.

### Cited Findings
- Locomotive’s 12 January 2022 essay (still linked in 2026) says hijacking is often a bad practice, that e-commerce should keep native scroll, and that some audiences “really require using the native scroll.” They do not ban it: experiential portfolios are the case they accept. Bad configuration “could create rendering and performance issues.” — [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20230221224510/https://medium.com/@LocomotiveMTL/should-i-use-locomotive-scroll-on-my-project-7fc8fa38bcc5)
- GSAP documents that with `scrub: true` (or a number), tween durations are proportions of the scroll distance, not seconds of playback. Their worked example: a 1s + 3s + 1s timeline over one viewport height plays the first tween from the 100% mark to the 80% mark, the second from 80% to 20%, the third from 20% to 0%, because 1/5 and 3/5 of a 5-second total. — [ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/) (section “How does duration work with scrub: true?”, read on the live docs page 2026-10-08; docs chrome showed v3.15)
- The same docs page’s timeline sample pins with `end: "+=500"` (500px past the start), `scrub: 1` described in the sample comment as smooth scrubbing that takes 1 second to catch up to the scrollbar, and `snap` to timeline labels. That is a short pin broken into named stages, not an open-ended scatter. — [ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/) (sample retrieved with the docs text)
- `toggleActions` controls four moments — onEnter, onLeave, onEnterBack, onLeaveBack — with keywords play, pause, resume, reset, restart, complete, reverse, none. The documented default is `play none none none`: the linked animation plays on the way in and is not rewound by scrolling back. — [ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/)
- `anticipatePin` exists because “most modern browsers handle scroll repaints on a separate thread,” so a fast scroll can paint one frame of unpinned content (they say perhaps 1/60th of a second). `anticipatePin: 1` is “typically fine”; the default is 0 and “in many cases you don’t need” it. — [ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/) (sentence beginning “If you pin large sections/panels…”, read on the live page)
- `pinSpacing` defaults to extra padding so content below does not slide under the pin. It is a bad fit for some layouts: if the parent is `display: flex`, GSAP sets `pinSpacing: false` by default because padding does not push flex siblings. `position: absolute` parents have the same problem. — [ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/)
- Darkroom’s 2026 work notes separate “scroll-driven work” (their Revelo plugin) from “Lenis for the smooth scroll” on the Stack project. Smooth scroll and the scroll-linked animation are named as two tools, not one gesture. — [darkroom.engineering/work](https://darkroom.engineering/work)

### Inferences
- “Don’t explode every word” follows from the scrub math, not from a studio slogan. If twenty words each travel on their own x/y across the whole timeline, every one of them is still mid-flight for most of the pin. The only moment the line can be read is progress ≈ 1. A visitor who flicks, or who meets a numeric `scrub` that is still catching up, leaves before the sentence exists. That is the pattern failing as a picture: a picture has to be legible while you are in it, not only on the last frame.
- One idea per viewport is the same math from the other side. GSAP’s own sample spends one viewport height on three sequential beats whose durations are 20% / 60% / 20% of that height, or pins for a fixed `+=500` and snaps to labels. The unit is a beat with a name, not a bag of letters sharing one pin.
- The HTML that could be read supports a different move than scatter-and-gather. Locomotive adds words onto a sentence that already has a place. Darkroom repeats one phrase until it is an identity. Obys lists names in order. Scroll, if it does anything, changes which phrase is current or how much of a stable line is visible. It does not invent the line from random coordinates.
- A long pin is not a style. Locomotive’s essay treats “stopping and starting the scroll” as the definition of hijacking, and says that power is often a bad practice outside experiential pieces. A personal page that holds the viewport hostage while words commute is the case they told people to justify, not the default.
- Scrub is the wrong tool for the first reading of a sentence. `toggleActions: "play none none none"` is a title that plays once in real time and then sits still so it can be read. Scrub is a score: position owns the frame. Use the score for a change the reader should be able to rewind (the next line replaces this one, a crop tightens, a second color arrives). Do not use it to assemble the words the reader needed in order to know what the page is.

### Gaps
- No opened studio article gives a pin length in pixels or viewports, or says “one idea per screen” in those words. The pin-length claim above is GSAP’s proportion model plus Locomotive’s hijacking warning, not a measured house style.
- Obys, Active Theory, Resn, and Aristide published no retrieved essay on kinetic type in this pass.
- Whether Locomotive’s 2026 hero scrubs or plays on load was not observed. The accumulating sentence is a DOM fact only.

## What goes wrong when ScrollTrigger pin/scrub is combined with Lenis?

### Takeaway
Current Lenis (README on main, stylesheet pinned in that README at unpkg `lenis@1.3.26`) says it runs on the browser’s own scroll, and its GSAP recipe is `lenis.on("scroll", ScrollTrigger.update)` plus the GSAP ticker calling `lenis.raf`, with `gsap.ticker.lagSmoothing(0)`. `scrollerProxy` does not appear in that README. It is documented by GSAP as a way to hijack a scroller’s `scrollTop`, and the Lenis maintainer told a user to use it when Lenis is not the root scroller. Wiring proxy and the ticker recipe together, or also turning on `normalizeScroll`, is how pins jump, progress sticks, and mobile scroll dies. A scrubbed word cloud adds a second failure: the readable frame is the last frame, and any lag means the words never arrive.

### Cited Findings
- Lenis README features, read from raw main: “Runs on native scroll — wraps the browser’s own scroll, so position: sticky, anchor links, and accessibility keep working.” The GSAP section tells you to listen with `lenis.on("scroll", ScrollTrigger.update)`, drive `lenis.raf` from `gsap.ticker` (ticker time is seconds, `raf` wants milliseconds), and call `gsap.ticker.lagSmoothing(0)` “to prevent any delay in scroll animations.” A separate basic setup uses `autoRaf: true` or a single `requestAnimationFrame` loop, not both. Troubleshooting says: use the latest version, include the recommended CSS (`lenis/dist/lenis.css` or the unpkg 1.3.26 file), “ensure proper integration,” test the page with Lenis removed, and actually call `lenis.raf`. The word `scrollerProxy` does not occur in that README. — [README](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/blob/main/README.md)
- GSAP `ScrollTrigger.scrollerProxy()` “allows you to hijack the scrollTop and/or scrollLeft getters/setters for a particular scroller element so that you can implement things like smooth scrolling or other custom effects.” Docs chrome v3.15. — [scrollerProxy()](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/static.scrollerProxy()/)
- Lenis maintainer clementroche, on closed issue #490 (non-root React Lenis, `root={false}`, user trying to feed a wrapper to ScrollTrigger): use `ScrollTrigger.scrollerProxy` on `lenisRef.current.wrapper`, with `scrollTop` reading and writing that wrapper. That advice is for a custom scroller, not for the window. The issue was closed for inactivity after the snippet. — [#490](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/490)
- Closed issue #112 (29 March 2023): a user scrubbing via `onUpdate` saw `self.progress` jump and run late against Lenis. clementroche: fixed in GSAP 3.11.5 if you call `ScrollTrigger.update()` from `lenis.on("scroll")`. Jack Doyle (GSAP): ScrollTrigger had been caching scroll values; `ScrollTrigger.update()` clears that cache. — [#112](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/112)
- Closed issue #233 (28 September 2023), title “lag when using position sticky (pin property in GSAP).” The reporter’s snippet started a `requestAnimationFrame` loop that called `lenis.raf` and also added a second `lenis.raf` on `gsap.ticker`, then pinned with `end: "+=1500"` and `scrub: 1`. Maintainer asked for a sandbox. The reporter later wrote that the problem was GSAP, not Lenis. The thread does not isolate a single root cause. The double `raf` is visible in the posted code and contradicts the README’s one-loop setup. — [#233](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/233)
- `normalizeScroll(true)` “forces scrolling to be done on the JavaScript thread, ensuring screen updates are synchronized and the address bar doesn’t show/hide on [most] mobile devices.” `false` leaves native scrolling, “often handled on a separate thread,” so repainting “may” be slightly out of sync. — [normalizeScroll()](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/static.normalizeScroll()/)
- Closed issue #274 (8 February 2024): with Lenis, `ScrollTrigger.normalizeScroll(true)` made Firefox on Windows “unresponsively slow.” clementroche: “Lenis already runs scroll in the main-thread, why would you need normalizeScroll from ScrollTrigger?” The reporter said pinned (and even unpinned) animations jump on mobile Chrome unless normalizeScroll is on, and that the jump also happened with Lenis disabled. Maintainer pointed at the normalizeScroll docs and `ScrollTrigger.config({ ignoreMobileResize: true })`. The reporter said the jump was not the address bar. So the thread records a real mobile jump, and also records that stacking normalizeScroll on Lenis has its own breakage, without a single agreed fix. — [#274](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/274)
- Closed issue #341 (30 May 2024): Lenis scroll “stops abruptly” on a real iPhone (iOS 17.5.1). One commenter could not reproduce, and noted horizontal overflow on a page. Another commenter, nye: the same stop happened with Lenis plus ScrollTrigger, and removing `ScrollTrigger.normalizeScroll(true)` fixed it. — [#341](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/341)
- Open issue #103: `position: fixed` elements jitter on macOS Safari on sites using Lenis. The README limitations still list this, narrowed: “position fixed seems to lag on MacOS Safari pre-M1,” linking that issue. — [#103](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/103); [README limitations](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/blob/main/README.md)
- Other limitations in the current README: no CSS scroll-snap (use `lenis/snap`); animation capped at 60fps on Safari and 30fps on low-power mode; smooth scroll stops over iframes because they do not forward wheel events; `syncTouch` “may behave unexpectedly” on iOS below 16; nested scrollers need `allowNestedScroll` or `data-lenis-prevent`, and the auto option “can create performance issues” because it walks the DOM on every scroll event. Anchor links are prevented while scrolling unless `anchors: true`. — [README](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/blob/main/README.md)
- Closed issue #419, from the issue search snippet only: setting `overflow: hidden` on `html` and `overflow-y: scroll` on `body` (a common mobile-nav scroll lock) stopped Lenis until the instance was pointed at the new wrapper. Full thread not read. — [#419](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/419)
- Closed issue #355, snippet only: a flex gallery that overflows horizontally made the page sideways-scrollable after a mobile resize; adding `overflow: hidden` then collided with vertical scroll. Full thread not read. — [#355](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/355)
- Closed issue #458, snippet only: `gestureOrientation: "both"` or `"vertical"` did not turn a vertical finger-swipe into horizontal scroll on mobile browsers; the desktop pen did. Full thread not read. — [#458](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/458)
- GSAP’s pin flash is the off-thread repaint described under `anticipatePin`. Lenis’s manifesto describes the same class of bug for WebGL: native scroll and a second renderer “don’t play nicely,” and Lenis exists to interpolate one scroll position both can share. Two clocks (Lenis lerp plus a scrub catch-up of 1 second, plus a proxied scrollTop) are three opinions about where the page is. — [anticipatePin on ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/); [MANIFESTO.md](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/blob/main/MANIFESTO.md)

### Inferences
- The portfolio’s “Lenis wired with scrollerProxy” matches the old virtual-scroller pattern and issue #490’s nested-wrapper case. It does not match the current root recipe. If Lenis is already moving the document’s real scroll, a proxy that also hijacks `scrollTop` can feed ScrollTrigger a different position than the one the pin spacer was built from. Symptom: the pin jumps, the spacer is the wrong height, progress sticks short of 1, words stop mid-air.
- “Words never arriving” is what numeric scrub means. `scrub: 1` is defined as a one-second catch-up. On a short pin, or a fast flick, the scrollbar hits the end while the words are still easing. Combined with #112’s cached progress (if `ScrollTrigger.update` is missing or is fighting a proxy), the timeline can sit below 1 forever.
- “Stuck progress” is the bug Jack Doyle described: ScrollTrigger will not notice Lenis’s interpolated position until `ScrollTrigger.update()` runs on the Lenis scroll event. The README’s `lagSmoothing(0)` is there because GSAP’s own ticker smoothing adds yet another delay.
- Calling `lenis.raf` from both `requestAnimationFrame` and `gsap.ticker`, as in the #233 snippet, steps the smooth scroll twice per frame. The README shows one or the other.
- `normalizeScroll(true)` is not a free pin fix on top of Lenis. The maintainer’s question in #274 is the warning. #341 records mobile scroll that stops until it is removed. #274 also records that mobile pin jumps can be a ScrollTrigger/browser problem even with Lenis off. Turning the flag on to hide a jump can trade it for a dead scrollbar, especially Firefox on Windows and some phones.
- Mobile overflow is the scattered-word layout itself. Words parked at large positive or negative x are outside the viewport. #355’s horizontal-overflow failure is the same shape: the page becomes sideways-scrollable, and `overflow: hidden` on the wrong element then fights Lenis. #419’s html/body overflow split is the other way a “lock the viewport while pinned” CSS habit kills scroll.
- `position: fixed` pins and fixed navs can jitter on Safari (issue #103, still listed as a limitation for pre-M1). A type stage that pins by fixing the sentence sits on that bug.
- None of these threads say the artistic failure (every word on its own offset) is a Lenis bug. Lenis can be in sync and the sentence can still be unreadable until the last percent, because that is how scrub proportions work.

### Gaps
- Issues #355, #419, #458, and #103 were not read past the opening report (and, for #103, the README’s one-line limitation). Resolutions, if any, are not summarized here.
- This pass did not read the portfolio’s Lenis/ScrollTrigger wiring, so it does not confirm that scrollerProxy is applied to `window` rather than a wrapper.
- No GitHub issue was found whose title is “words never arrive.” That phrase is the scrub catch-up plus the #112 cache, not a quoted bug report.
- No measurement was made of a specific `end` distance or `scrub` value in a studio codebase.

## How do they handle reduced motion and small screens?

### Takeaway
Lenis, as of the current README, turns smoothing off when `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is set, and keeps the scroll position in sync. GSAP’s current responsive API is `gsap.matchMedia()` (the older `ScrollTrigger.matchMedia()` is marked deprecated). No opened studio page stated a small-screen pin policy. The failure reports say mobile pins jump, horizontal overflow steals the gesture, and a vertical swipe does not drive a horizontal Lenis scene.

### Cited Findings
- Lenis README, “Reduced motion”: by default, if `prefers-reduced-motion` is `reduce`, smoothing is disabled (`lerp` forced to `1`, so scroll tracks the device 1:1, ignoring `duration` and `easing`) and `scrollTo` / anchor scrolls jump. Lenis keeps running “so WebGL/DOM synchronization stays intact,” and the preference updates without a reload. `lenis.prefersReducedMotion` is exposed so other animations can follow. Opting out with `respectReducedMotion: false` is “not recommended.” — [README](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/blob/main/README.md)
- `ScrollTrigger.matchMedia()` is marked deprecated on the current ScrollTrigger docs. `ScrollTrigger.clearMatchMedia()` says to move to `gsap.matchMedia()` as of GSAP 3.11.0+. — [ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/); [clearMatchMedia()](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/static.clearMatchMedia%28%29)
- `gsap.matchMedia()` example: named conditions `isDesktop`, `isMobile`, and `reduceMotion: "(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)"`. The sample sets a tween’s duration to `0` when `reduceMotion` is true, and to `2` otherwise, and uses a shorter rotation on the mobile condition. The handler reverts and reruns when a condition flips. — [gsap.matchMedia()](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.matchMedia%28%29) (docs text retrieved via Context7; this exact URL was not opened in the browser fetch)
- Locomotive, January 2022: some projects “really require using the native scroll” because of accessibility, and hijacking breaks find-in-page. They do not mention `prefers-reduced-motion` by name. — [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20230221224510/https://medium.com/@LocomotiveMTL/should-i-use-locomotive-scroll-on-my-project-7fc8fa38bcc5)
- Mobile pin/jump and gesture reports are the ones already cited: #274 (jumps on mobile Chrome, normalizeScroll makes Firefox desktop unusable), #341 (scroll stops on device; horizontal overflow; removing normalizeScroll helped one user), #355 (horizontal overflow after mobile resize), #458 (vertical swipe does not move a horizontal Lenis scene on mobile), README limits on `syncTouch` below iOS 16 and on Safari frame rate. — [#274](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/274); [#341](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/341); [#355](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/355); [#458](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/issues/458); [README](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/blob/main/README.md)
- Darkroom’s about page says they care about “craft, speed, accessibility, and systems that remain maintainable after launch.” It does not describe a reduced-motion type fallback. — [About](https://darkroom.engineering/about)

### Inferences
- Reduced motion, on the Lenis authors’ defaults, means the page is already in its final reading state and scroll is 1:1. A sentence that only becomes a sentence at scrub progress 1 fails that default: turning lerp off does not help if the words are still absolutely placed at their “from” coordinates until the timeline ends. The GSAP sample’s `duration: 0` is the same idea for a time-based tween: skip to the end. For a scrubbed scene, the equivalent is not a faster scrub. It is to not scrub, and to render the settled line.
- Small screens are where this experiment’s layout breaks even if the desktop pin is perfect. Off-screen x/y words are horizontal overflow (#355). A pin that assumes a tall desktop viewport becomes a trap on a short phone, and #274 says those pins already jump. A horizontal word-marquee will not follow a vertical thumb unless gesture mapping is deliberate, and #458 says Lenis does not do that mapping for you on mobile.
- Studios were not shown, in any opened page, to “disable all type motion under 800px.” The evidence stops at: Lenis respects reduced motion by killing smoothing; GSAP expects you to branch with `gsap.matchMedia()`; mobile pin + extra scroll normalizers are a known fight.

- The `gsap.matchMedia()` code sample was not re-fetched from the live HTML in this pass; it comes from the docs retrieval cited above. Deprecation of `ScrollTrigger.matchMedia()` was on the ScrollTrigger docs page that was opened.

## What is the difference between a title sequence and a scroll score?

### Takeaway
A title sequence is time-based and plays once: GSAP’s default `toggleActions` is `play none none none`, and durations are seconds. A scroll score is scrubbed: progress is a fraction of the distance between `start` and `end`, durations are ratios of that distance, and scrolling back rewinds the score. A numeric scrub is a hybrid that lags the score by a catch-up time, which is why a flicked score never lands. The first reading of a name should be a title sequence. Scroll should score the next idea.

### Cited Findings
- Without scrub, `toggleActions` decides what happens at onEnter, onLeave, onEnterBack, and onLeaveBack. Default `play none none none` plays forward once when the trigger enters and does not reset when you scroll away. Keywords include `restart`, `reverse`, `reset`, and `complete`. — [ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/)
- With `scrub: true` or `scrub` as a number, those durations stop being time. They are shares of the scroll range. The docs’ 1s/3s/1s example over one viewport height is the definition. The sample comment on `scrub: 1` calls it smooth scrubbing that takes one second to catch up to the scrollbar, which means the animation is no longer a pure function of scroll position. — [ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/)
- The same sample pins, then snaps to labels (`start`, `color`, `spin`, `end`) with a snap duration between 0.2 and 3 seconds depending on velocity. Snap is a third mode: the score pulls you to a named frame instead of leaving you between words. — [ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/)
- Lenis reduced-motion mode makes programmatic scrolls instant and sets lerp to 1. A time-based title can honor that by finishing immediately (the matchMedia sample’s duration 0). A long scrub cannot “play faster”; the user has to supply the distance. — [Lenis README](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/blob/main/README.md); [gsap.matchMedia()](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.matchMedia%28%29)
- Locomotive’s essay separates “entry/leave animation with or without delay” from “advanced animations relative to the scroll (possibly with GSAP).” They already talk about the two tools as different jobs. The essay is from 12 January 2022. — [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20230221224510/https://medium.com/@LocomotiveMTL/should-i-use-locomotive-scroll-on-my-project-7fc8fa38bcc5)

### Inferences
- A title sequence is allowed to be cinematic because it ends. The visitor waits a short, fixed time and then reads. It is the right instrument for a name, a role, one sentence. It should not be pinned, and it should not depend on scroll position, or a reduced-motion user and a trackpad flick never get the sentence.
- A scroll score is allowed to be slow because the visitor is steering, and because reversing is the point. It is the right instrument for a change of state that has a before and an after: this line leaves, the next line is the picture; a paragraph crops; a list advances by one project. The settled state of each beat has to be readable in the middle of the beat, not only at progress 1.
- The current experiment is a scroll score used as a title sequence. The words start scattered, so the title does not exist until the score ends. That inverts both tools. The fix in kind, not in code: put the line down as a title (time-based, once, already in place), and if scroll continues, score one subsequent change — the next thought, not the birth of the letters.
- Snap-to-labels is the studio-shaped version of a score when you do pin: a few named frames, each a complete sentence, rather than a continuous interpolation of twenty coordinates. GSAP’s sample is built that way. Nothing opened here shows a prize-winning page interpolating a bag of free-floating words for several viewports.
- “Scroll score” and “title sequence” are not terms found in the Lenis or Locomotive texts. They are names for the two GSAP behaviors above (`scrub` versus `toggleActions`). Do not attribute the nicknames to those studios.

### Gaps
- No motion-design essay from 2023–2026 was retrieved that uses the words “title sequence” and “scroll score.” The distinction here is the GSAP model, checked on the docs, plus Locomotive’s 2022 split between enter animations and scroll-relative animations.
- No studio was watched long enough to time their pin or count their beats. The “one subsequent change” recommendation is an inference from the scrub-proportion docs and from the type-led HTML that was readable, not a measured house rule from Obys or Active Theory.
