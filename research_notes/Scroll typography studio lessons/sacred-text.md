# A whole sacred sentence, not flying words

Scope: the Diamond Sutra sentence the user wrote as 凡所有相皆是虚妄, one Bible verse that can sit beside it without being treated as the same claim, how Chinese typography keeps a sentence as one visual unit, and real pages where a whole line moves. Scripture below is quoted only from pages or files retrieved for this note. ctext.org did not yield a text.

## What is the full classical sentence, and how is it punctuated?

### Takeaway

The user’s 凡所有相皆是虚妄 is the first clause only, in simplified characters, with no punctuation. In the standard electronic Taishō text of Kumārajīva (CBETA T235), the whole utterance is one punctuated sentence, traditional characters, with 則 not 即: 「凡所有相，皆是虛妄；若見諸相非相，則見如來。」 The form with a full stop after 虛妄 and 即見如來 is a different edition, not that Taishō reading.

### Cited Findings

- CBETA’s TEI file is titled 大正新脩大藏經數位版, No. 235 金剛般若波羅蜜經, author 後秦 鳩摩羅什譯, file date 2026-02-09. The encoding description says the punctuated text was provided by the Electronic Buddhadharma Society and re-proofed by 張革豐, and `editorialDecl` labels the punctuation 新式標點. At Taishō line 0749a24–a25 the text reads: 「凡所有相，皆是虛妄；若見諸相非相，則見如來。」 The file contains 則見 once and 即見 zero times. Line 0749a24 ends 則見 and line 0749a25 begins 如來。」 — [CBETA XML, T08n0235](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cbeta-org/xml-p5/master/T/T08/T08n0235.xml); repository path [T/T08/T08n0235.xml](https://github.com/cbeta-org/xml-p5/blob/master/T/T08/T08n0235.xml)
- The human-facing reader https://cbetaonline.dila.edu.tw/zh/T0235 returned HTTP 200, but the HTML is an application shell (“CBETA 線上閱讀 Loading...”) and does not contain the sentence. The wording above is from the XML that reader is built on, not from the rendered page. — [CBETA Online Reader](https://cbetaonline.dila.edu.tw/zh/T0235)
- Chinese Wikisource’s page 金剛般若波羅蜜經 (鳩摩羅什) both claims `from = 大正新脩大藏經` and says in the notes that the text is 民國江味農居士校定本（依唐柳公權書於燉煌石室之版本）. In 如理實見分第五 it prints: 佛告須菩提：「凡所有相，皆是虛妄。若見諸相非相，即見如來。」 That page has 即見如來 once and 則見如來 zero times. This does not match the CBETA Taishō sentence (semicolon vs full stop; 則 vs 即). — [Wikisource, 鳩摩羅什 text](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E9%87%91%E5%89%9B%E8%88%AC%E8%8B%A5%E6%B3%A2%E7%BE%85%E8%9C%9C%E7%B6%93_(%E9%B3%A9%E6%91%A9%E7%BE%85%E4%BB%80))

### Inferences

- “The whole sentence” for design is the Kumārajīva utterance through 如來, not the eight-character fragment. Counted from the CBETA punctuated string, that is 22 character squares: 凡所有相，皆是虛妄；若見諸相非相，則見如來。 CBETA’s single final 。 treats it as one sentence; the semicolon joins the two clauses. The user’s string drops the second clause, the comma, the semicolon, and the full stop, and writes simplified 虚 instead of the traditional 虛 in the CBETA file.
- Do not silently “correct” the Taishō line to 即見如來. That reading is real on the Wikisource Jiang Weinong page, and 即 appears in other translators’ different sentences (Paramārtha 即是真實, Yijing 即非虛妄). It is not the CBETA T235 reading. A full stop after 皆是虛妄 is also Paramārtha’s punctuation, but Paramārtha continues 無所有相，即是真實, not 若見諸相非相，則見如來. Mixing those memories produces a sentence no one source prints.
- The punctuation is modern editorial punctuation on top of the canonical characters, not a claim about medieval dots. The characters to trust for Taishō Kumārajīva are 凡所有相皆是虛妄若見諸相非相則見如來. The punctuation to trust, if the design follows this electronic edition, is the comma, the semicolon, and one full stop as printed in the XML.
- Wikisource’s header says “Taishō” while its notes and its 即 / full-stop text disagree with CBETA’s Taishō XML. For this sentence, CBETA is the better Taishō witness. Wikisource is evidence of the popular Jiang recension, and it should be labeled as that if it is used.

### Gaps

- No Taishō print facsimile and no Dunhuang transcription of this sentence were retrieved. British Library and IDP URLs returned HTTP 403, including the IDP article cited by Wikipedia. The 868 scroll is documented as a five-metre printed scroll; how this particular sentence sits in its columns was not verified.
- ctext.org was blocked. The CBETA reader page did not contain the text in its initial HTML.
- Whether every manuscript behind the witnesses listed in the T235 header (宋, 元, 明, 宮, 麗, 磧) reads 則 was not checked. The XML as read has no apparatus note on this sentence; that is not a survey of every witness.

## Which one Bible verse can stand next to that line without a cheap equivalence?

### Takeaway

Use 2 Corinthians 4:18, quoted from the ESV as Bible Gateway serves it. It is about looking past what is seen because the seen does not last. It does not say that visible marks are empty, and it does not say that seeing them as non-marks is seeing the Tathagata. The other candidates are worse because each is about a different subject and is easier to fake as a translation of the sutra.

### Cited Findings

- ESV, 2 Corinthians 4:18, Bible Gateway: “as we look not to the things that are seen but to the things that are unseen. For the things that are seen are transient, but the things that are unseen are eternal.” The verse is printed with a lowercase “as” because it continues 4:17. ESV 4:17 on the same site: “For this light momentary affliction is preparing for us an eternal weight of glory beyond all comparison,”. Together, Bible Gateway’s meta description for 4:17–18 reads: “For this light momentary affliction is preparing for us an eternal weight of glory beyond all comparison, as we look not to the things that are seen but to the things that are unseen. For the things that are seen are transient, but the things that are unseen are eternal.” — [ESV 4:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Corinthians%204%3A18&version=ESV); [ESV 4:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Corinthians%204%3A17&version=ESV); [ESV 4:17–18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Corinthians%204%3A17-18&version=ESV)
- The same verse in other standard translations, same method (Bible Gateway `og:description`, except NRSV 1989 from oremus). NIV: “So we fix our eyes not on what is seen, but on what is unseen, since what is seen is temporary, but what is unseen is eternal.” KJV: “While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal.” A request for `version=NRSV` came back as NRSVUE: “because we look not at what can be seen but at what cannot be seen, for what can be seen is temporary, but what cannot be seen is eternal.” Oremus’s 1989 NRSV has a semicolon: “because we look not at what can be seen but at what cannot be seen; for what can be seen is temporary, but what cannot be seen is eternal.” — [NIV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Corinthians%204%3A18&version=NIV); [KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Corinthians%204%3A18&version=KJV); [NRSVUE on Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Corinthians%204%3A18&version=NRSVUE); [NRSV 1989 on oremus](https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=2+Corinthians+4:18&version=nrsv)
- ESV 1 Corinthians 13:12: “For now we see in a mirror dimly, but then face to face. Now I know in part; then I shall know fully, even as I have been fully known.” — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Corinthians%2013%3A12&version=ESV)
- ESV Ecclesiastes 1:2: “Vanity of vanities, says the Preacher, vanity of vanities! All is vanity.” — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ecclesiastes%201%3A2&version=ESV)
- ESV John 1:1. Bible Gateway’s meta description prefixes the section heading. The heading is “The Word Became Flesh”; the verse after the hyphen is: “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.” — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%201%3A1&version=ESV)
- ESV Hebrews 11:1. The meta description prefixes “By Faith.” The verse is: “Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen.” ESV Hebrews 11:3, with no heading glued on: “By faith we understand that the universe was created by the word of God, so that what is seen was not made out of things that are visible.” — [Hebrews 11:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%2011%3A1&version=ESV); [Hebrews 11:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%2011%3A3&version=ESV)

### Inferences

- Recommend 2 Corinthians 4:18 ESV, and if the screen needs a line that can stand alone, use the second sentence only: “For the things that are seen are transient, but the things that are unseen are eternal.” Still cite it as 4:18, and do not drop the first half if the citation is meant to be the whole verse. NIV 4:18 is already one sentence, if a second wording of this same verse is needed. It is not a different recommendation.
- Why this verse and not the others. It shares a neighborhood with 相: both texts refuse to stop at what appears. The claims are not the same. In the ESV, 4:17–18 is about light affliction preparing an eternal weight of glory, so the reader looks to what is unseen because the seen is transient and the unseen is eternal. The sutra sentence says that whatever has 相 is 虛妄, and that seeing marks as non-marks is seeing the Tathagata. Paul is not talking about 如來. The sutra is not talking about present suffering and future glory. Set them as neighbors. Do not caption one with the other.
- Ecclesiastes 1:2 is the worst pairing. “All is vanity” is the line most easily forced into a fake equation with 虛妄. Qohelet’s line is a verdict on everything, in the preacher’s voice. It does not say that marks are non-marks, and it does not say how one sees the Tathagata.
- 1 Corinthians 13:12 is about partial knowledge now and face-to-face knowledge later. “We see in a mirror dimly” is not “all marks are illusory.”
- John 1:1 is about the Word who was with God and was God. Beside 則見如來 it invites an identity the two sentences do not state.
- Hebrews 11:1 defines faith as conviction about what is not seen. The overlap is the word “unseen,” not the claim. Hebrews 11:3 is a creation sentence: what is seen was made by God’s word out of what is not visible. That is almost the opposite gesture from “marks are 虛妄.” It says the visible world comes from God’s word. It does not say appearances are empty.

### Gaps

- The verse texts were taken from Bible Gateway’s meta descriptions (and oremus for 1989 NRSV), not from a typeset Crossway page body. ESV.org’s chapter page was fetched, but its meta description was the start of 2 Corinthians 4, not verse 18 in isolation, so it was not used as the quote. John 1:1 and Hebrews 11:1 required stripping a section heading that Bible Gateway concatenates into the description; the heading is identified above so it is not quoted as scripture.
- No Greek text was fetched. The recommendation does not depend on a Greek gloss.

## How does Chinese web typography treat a full sentence as one visual unit, and why is splitting 凡所有相 a reading failure?

### Takeaway

The composition unit is the character square, set solid, with fullwidth punctuation glued to the clause. A normal book line is about 17–40 characters, so the 22-square sutra sentence is already one line. Characters may be broken only at square boundaries, and ，；。 may not start a line. Sending 凡, 所, 有, and 相 on separate paths is not “using the character as the unit.” It destroys the phrase the squares are there to tile.

### Cited Findings

- “The majority of the text used in Chinese composition consists of Han characters (Hanzi).” Traditional forms are the ones commonly used in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macao; simplified forms are the ones commonly used in mainland China, Singapore, and Malaysia. Punctuation marks are part of Chinese composition along with the characters. — [CLReq, characters used](https://w3c.github.io/clreq/#characters_used_in_chinese_composition)
- Characters and punctuation “have basically been designed to have a square character frame,” so the same types work vertically or horizontally by changing direction, with adjustments for punctuation. — [CLReq, writing modes](https://w3c.github.io/clreq/#writing_modes_in_chinese_composition)
- Vertical writing: characters top to bottom, lines right to left. Horizontal writing: characters left to right, lines top to bottom. Traditionally, a horizontal line inside a vertical text (a plaque, a newspaper title) ran right to left; “this has largely been replaced by the horizontal lines that are read from left to right.” — [CLReq, horizontal vs vertical](https://w3c.github.io/clreq/#major_differences_between_horizontal_and_vertical_writing_modes)
- “When composing a line with characters, in principle, no extra spacing appears between their character frames. This is called solid setting” (密排). Extra inter-character spacing (疏排) is a special case: balancing short running heads, short captions, “poetry with lines of only a few characters,” or children’s books. Those cases still describe a line with even added space, not characters on independent trajectories. — [CLReq, arranging characters](https://w3c.github.io/clreq/#principles_of_arrangement_of_han_characters); [CLReq, increased spacing](https://w3c.github.io/clreq/#increased_inter_character_spacing)
- Line length is set in characters per line. “Most books have line lengths of body text in the range between 17 to 40 characters.” Body text “should not be less than 10 characters” or breaks become hard to follow. Horizontal body copy should not exceed 48 characters; vertical body copy should not exceed 55. — [CLReq, type-area considerations](https://w3c.github.io/clreq/#considerations_in_designing_type_area)
- Basic line-start rules, called “the most recommended method”: commas, semicolons, colons, periods, exclamation marks, question marks, and closing quotes and brackets “should not appear at the line start.” Opening quotes and brackets should not appear at the line end. The Chinese text lists 顿号、逗号、句号、冒号、分号、叹号、问号 among the marks that 不能出现在一行的开头. — [CLReq, line-start and line-end rules](https://w3c.github.io/clreq/#prohibition_rules_for_line_start_end)
- 。 ends a sentence. ， separates parts of a sentence, such as clauses. ； separates major sentence elements. — [CLReq, punctuation categories](https://w3c.github.io/clreq/#categories_and_usage_of_punctuation_marks)
- The published URL https://www.w3.org/TR/clreq/ returned HTTP 403. The quotations above are from the editor’s draft at w3c.github.io/clreq, which is the `gh-pages` file of the w3c/clreq repository. Byte-for-byte identity with the blocked TR was not checked. — [draft](https://w3c.github.io/clreq/); [raw HTML](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/w3c/clreq/gh-pages/index.html); [TR, 403](https://www.w3.org/TR/clreq/)
- Chen Hui Jing: “Chinese is a logographic writing system, whereby each character itself holds meaning, whether alone or as part of a phrase.” Traditionally characters “were written from top-to-bottom, right-to-left.” She documents CSS `writing-mode` for that: vertical mode renders text top to bottom with a right-to-left block flow. Her vertical demo string is the phrase 从1987到现在, set as a run, not as loose letters. — [Chinese language on the web](https://chenhuijing.com/blog/chinese-web-typography/)
- The Chinese Copywriting Guidelines put a space at the boundary with English or numerals, and they forbid a space between fullwidth punctuation and adjacent characters. Correct: `刚刚买了一部 iPhone，好开心！` Wrong: a space before or after the comma. They also require fullwidth Chinese punctuation rather than `! ? "`. They never insert spaces between hanzi. — [README.zh-Hans.md](https://github.com/sparanoid/chinese-copywriting-guidelines/blob/master/README.zh-Hans.md)
- https://www.typeisbeautiful.com/ redirected to https://www.thetype.com/. The Type’s English about page says it was founded in 2007 as a project on typography, design, and culture. No article from that site about this sentence, or about forbidding per-character animation, was retrieved. — [thetype.com](https://www.thetype.com/); [About](https://www.thetype.com/en/about/)

### Inferences

- “Character as the unit” means each hanzi or fullwidth mark occupies one em square and the squares tile. It does not mean each character is a Latin letter that can be tracked, delayed, or flown on its own. Solid setting is the default. Loose setting is for a short line that is still one line.
- The CBETA sentence is 22 squares. That sits inside the 17–40 character book measure, above the 10-character floor, and under both the 48-character horizontal cap and the 55-character vertical cap. The ordinary setting is one horizontal line, or one vertical column. Measure does not ask for a break, and it does not ask for sixteen separate objects.
- A legal line break, if one is forced, is between squares, and ， ； 。 「 must obey the line-start / line-end rules. The comma after 凡所有相 is the pause between the subject phrase and 皆是虛妄. Delete it, and the only mark of that pause is gone. In the CBETA lineation, 0749a25 begins with 如來, so the electronic Taishō line break keeps the two-character name together. Animating 如 and 來 apart would split a word that this line break does not split.
- 凡所有相 is one classical noun phrase: 凡所有 modifies 相. 所 does not stand as its own statement. 所有 is the unit “whatever there is,” and 相 is the noun those characters qualify. 虛妄 is a two-character word. 如來 is a two-character name. Chen’s point that a character means something “alone or as part of a phrase” cuts both ways: in this sentence the phrase is the reading. Four flying tokens 凡 / 所 / 有 / 相 read as four slogans. That is a reading failure, not a finer use of the em square.
- Simplified 虚妄 is the regional simplified spelling CLReq describes, not a second sentence. The CBETA text uses traditional 虛. A classical line set from that file should stay in traditional characters. Do not mix 虚 into the Taishō sentence and call it the same transcription.

### Gaps

- No page from The Type / Type is Beautiful was found that discusses this sutra line or kinetic hanzi. The studio is cited only as the current home of the suggested site.
- GB/T 15834—2011 is named by CLReq as the mainland standard for punctuation position. The standard’s own text was not fetched.
- Whether a 22-character vertical column matches any particular sutra panel (17 characters, 20, and so on) was not measured from a facsimile.

## Which real sites and title sequences move a whole line instead of scattering words?

### Takeaway

The verified motion examples are title sequences in which the moving unit is a title card or a flowing line: Saul Bass’s *North by Northwest*, and Kuntzel + Deygas’s *Catch Me If You Can*. SuttaCentral’s data model and the Poetry Foundation poem page keep literary text at sentence or line size, but they are not motion. A museum page that animates this sutra sentence, or any sacred sentence, as one moving line was not retrieved.

### Cited Findings

- Art of the Title on *North by Northwest* (1959), Saul Bass: the page’s description is “With bold lines and moving type, Saul Bass ushers the title sequence into the modern era.” The article says Bass’s earlier title cards “had always remained static,” and that *North by Northwest* “is often credited as being the first sequence to use kinetic type – or simply, type in motion.” It also calls the sequence an early case of “situational type,” text integrated into the scene by matching its perspective. — [Art of the Title, North by Northwest](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/north-by-northwest/)
- Art of the Title on *Catch Me If You Can* (2002), Kuntzel + Deygas: “Flowing type, smooth lines and cool jazz are a playground for this pursuit, snaking and sneaking across the colorful jet-set world … slowly fading to reveal the darkened truth.” The designers describe the sequence as made under conditions they compare to 1960s title artists, and as a stylistic transposition of Saul Bass. — [Art of the Title, Catch Me If You Can](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/catch-me-if-you-can/)
- The Poetry Foundation’s page for Robert Frost’s “The Road Not Taken” presents the poem as lines. The opening line on that page is “Two roads diverged in a yellow wood,”. Nothing on the fetched page describes scroll motion or per-word animation. — [Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/44272/the-road-not-taken)
- SuttaCentral’s HTML for SN 56.11 says “Please enable JavaScript to view this website,” so the bilingual layout was not seen. The public Bilara JSON behind it is keyed by segment, not by word. Sample values: `sn56.11:1.1` “At one time the Buddha was staying near Varanasi in the deer park at Isipatana.”; `sn56.11:1.2` “There the Buddha addressed the group of five mendicants:”; `sn56.11:2.1` “Mendicants, these two extremes should not be cultivated by one gone forth.” The file has 60 such segments, including short headings. — [SuttaCentral page, JS shell](https://suttacentral.net/sn56.11/en/sujato); [Bilara JSON](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/suttacentral/bilara-data/published/translation/en/sujato/sutta/sn/sn56/sn56.11_translation-en-sujato.json); [segment API](https://suttacentral.net/api/bilarasuttas/sn56.11/sujato)
- 84000’s Diamond Sūtra reading-room URL resolved to a page titled “The Sūtra on the Perfection of Wisdom ‘The Diamond Cutter’ (The Diamond Sūtra).” The HTML received was navigation chrome, not the verse layout, so how a verse moves or breaks was not verified. — [84000, Toh 16](https://84000.co/translation/toh16)
- The Guggenheim’s Jenny Holzer artist page returned a body that says the site does not work properly without JavaScript. Wikipedia’s intro says her work “focuses on the delivery of words and ideas in public spaces” and includes “electronic visual displays.” That does not say whether a sentence stays intact while it moves. She is not used as an example. — [Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/artwork/artist/jenny-holzer); [Wikipedia, Jenny Holzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Holzer)
- A long guessed Art of the Title URL for *Dr. Strangelove* returned HTTP 404. The shorter slug was not successfully retrieved in this pass. — [404](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/dr-strangelove-or-how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying-and-love-the-bomb/)

### Inferences

- The motion that matches these sources is motion of a line or a title card: the card travels, the line flows, the line fades, perspective locks the whole line to a surface. Bass’s article specifically contrasts static title cards with type in motion. The thing that used to sit still, and then moves, is the card.
- For the sutra, that means one solid run of 「凡所有相，皆是虛妄；若見諸相非相，則見如來。」 can be masked, tracked as a single run, shifted on its baseline, or replaced by the next whole line (for example the ESV sentence “For the things that are seen are transient, but the things that are unseen are eternal.”). Replacement is succession of two sentences. It is not a claim that they say the same thing.
- SuttaCentral is the relevant bilingual model even without motion: one segment, one clause or sentence, aligned as a unit. Poetry Foundation is the relevant literary model: the line is the box. Neither site, as fetched, scatters words, and neither site was seen animating them.
- *Catch Me If You Can* is cited only for “flowing type” and “smooth lines.” The article does not explicitly say “no letter is detached.” Treat “the line is the moving object” as a reading of those phrases, not as a frame-by-frame log.

### Gaps

- No working museum, poetry, or bilingual URL was found that both displays a sacred sentence and animates that sentence as one line. British Library and IDP returned 403. The Guggenheim Holzer page and the SuttaCentral reader need JavaScript. 84000’s fetched HTML did not include the verse block.
- No CSS case-study page (mask reveal, baseline shift) was fetched from a design studio. The title-sequence articles are the motion evidence. They are film sequences with real URLs, not live web type.
- *Anatomy of a Murder* was fetched and not used. Art of the Title describes Bass taking a body apart beside crew names. That is disassembly of an image, and it is the wrong metaphor for this sentence, so it is not offered as a model. — [Art of the Title, Anatomy of a Murder](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/anatomy-of-a-murder/)
